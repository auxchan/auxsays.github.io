### AUX-001 — One patch published two contradicting decisions

- **Status** Resolved · **Area** Patch decision · **Severity** High
- **First seen** 2026-09-16 · **Resolved** 2026-09-16
- **PR** #142 · **Merge** `bc5d1a19` · **Production** `962f211c`

**Symptom** `2026-04-14-davinci-resolve-21-public-beta-1.md` published `WAIT for production systems`
as its verdict and `AVOID for production` as its summary. Both reached readers — the patch page
renders the verdict, the `/updates/` feed renders the summary — so no single page showed the
conflict. 10 of 1,168 records named more than one action.

**Root cause** Three independent implementations of one install/wait/avoid policy:
`_record_coherence_fields` (product policy), `_public_summary` → `_recommendation_prefix`
(sentiment, with its own beta rule), and `build_consensus_from_evidence.recommendation_prefix`.

**Resolution** `lib/patch_decision.py` is the only place a decision is chosen.
`_proposed_record_fields` calls it once per record and hands the same string to both writers.
`_recommendation_prefix` deleted, not delegated. Also closed: the two paths judged different
version strings (`ver` vs `patch_display_label(...)`).

**Verification** 212 evidence groups A/B'd, baseline writer vs patched: 9 groups changed, all only
in `update_consensus_summary`, all already self-contradicting; records naming two actions 9 → 0.
CI 89 suites / 4,996 checks. Production regeneration repaired 9 records; `qa_patch_records` went
9 warnings → 0 across 1,168 records; both live surfaces verified in agreement.

**Known residuals** AUX-006, AUX-007, AUX-008, AUX-009 all originate here.

**Reopen only if** conflicting verdict actions appear on a record again; a second recommendation
authority is introduced; or production regeneration produces divergence between the record writer
and `build_consensus_from_evidence`.

**Evidence** `auxsays/scripts/tests/test_patch_decision_authority.py` (47 governed checks, 7
mutants); `qa_patch_records.record_names_conflicting_actions`.

### AUX-005 — Official hard-stop AVOID path (partially built)

- **Status** Deferred · **Area** Patch decision · **Severity** Medium · **First seen** 2026-09-23
- **Branch** `sprint/official-hard-stop-avoid` · **WIP commit** `9fdbcbdb` (not merged, not a PR)

**Symptom / gap** AVOID remains a canonical AUXSAYS verdict but is currently unreachable from
`decision_label()`, so the product cannot answer "avoid" even when a vendor withdraws a build.

**Approved policy** Production AVOID uses **official hard-stop semantics only**: the exact
patch/build was withdrawn or recalled, the vendor explicitly says do not install it, or the vendor
explicitly recommends rollback/uninstall over a serious regression. Not inferred from ordinary
known issues, sentiment, report count, classifier severity, issue theme, beta status or generic
safeguard language. A cohort-scoped safeguard hold must **not** globally convert a patch to AVOID;
holds stay as structured official risk evidence.

**State of the work** Landed on the branch: `patch_decision.official_hard_stop()` reading a
structured `official_install_block` (fails closed on non-dict, non-`active`, unrecognised `type`,
or any scope that is not explicitly `global`), AVOID placed above the count check, a
product-agnostic hard-stop branch in `_record_coherence_fields` that survives a zero community
count, and a hard-stop summary. **Not done:** threading the state through
`build_consensus_from_evidence.record_status_index` so all three call sites agree; retraction-fence
protection (`ZERO_COUNT_PROJECTION_FIELDS` deletes `update_decision_label` at zero count); the
zero-report override, which forces `INSUFFICIENT DATA` in **two** places —
`_layouts/aux-update.html:288-290` and `_includes/patch-table-row.html:32` — and so would erase an
AVOID on both the detail page and the product table; a QA exemption; tests; governed manifest count.

**Note for whoever resumes** A patch withdrawn on release day has zero reports by definition, which
puts it squarely in that override's population (994 of 1,200 records at `2cb56aee`). Getting the
authority right is necessary and not sufficient — the templates decide what the reader sees. Also
confirmed: no existing field can carry this state. `official_safeguard_hold_count` is the nearest,
and it is `0` on all 15 records that carry it, has never once matched its extractor, is scoped per
feature-version rather than per build, and is cohort-shaped — the exact thing that must not convert
a patch globally.

**Known residual (design)** The promotion work-list is built from evidence rows, so a record with a
hard stop and zero community reports is never visited by the writer. Closing that needs the
work-list to union in records carrying an active block.

**Why deferred** No producer of `official_install_block` exists, no tracked build is currently
withdrawn, and the expected corpus outcome is 0 AVOID records. Nothing is broken in production.

**Current impact** None. AVOID is unreachable; no record claims otherwise.

**Revisit trigger** A vendor withdraws, recalls or issues a do-not-install/rollback directive for a
tracked build; or a producer for the structured field is proposed.

**Must not block** Any roadmap work. This is additive.

### AUX-006 — Community-triggered AVOID

- **Status** Deferred · **Area** Patch decision · **Severity** Medium

**Gap** Community evidence cannot emit AVOID and will not for this implementation generation.

**Why deferred** The limitations are structural, not a missing threshold: `severity: critical` is
classifier-derived; severity, issue theme and sentiment are returned by a single classifier branch,

which time the patch is usually superseded.

**Current impact** None. AVOID is simply not community-reachable.

**Revisit trigger** All of: structured harm classification, trustworthy severity extraction,
cross-product support, independent corroboration, adequate source-family depth.

**Must not block** Any roadmap work. Do not invent a community threshold in the meantime.

### AUX-010 — Site-wide heading contrast

- **Status** Resolved · **Area** Presentation · **Severity** Medium

**Symptom** The AUX-004 defect remained on every layout except patch pages: home, feed, vendor,
product, methodology and about all rendered headings at 1.01–1.23:1 in OS light mode.

**Root cause** AUXSAYS paints a fixed dark ground in every colour scheme, but Chirpy colours h1–h5
with `var(--heading-color)` and flips that token on `prefers-color-scheme` (#2a2a2a light,
#cccccc dark). AUX-004 took ownership of the token for patch pages only, leaving every other
layout reading the theme's light-mode value on the dark ground.

**Repair** `body.aux-page { --heading-color: var(--text-0); }` — `aux-base.html` emits
`<body class="aux-page {{ page.layout }}">` for every AUXSAYS layout, so that selector is exactly
the AUXSAYS-owned ground. It replaces the narrower `body.aux-update` rule (same weight, same
value). `vault.html` renders `body.vault-page` and keeps the theme's behaviour.

**Verification** Production, both OS schemes, desktop and 390px: home 15.81:1, feed 15.81:1,
vendor 6.43:1, product 6.43:1, methodology 15.81:1, about 9.09:1, patch page 15.81:1 — all at the
same `#ece7dd` in either scheme. Setting the token back to the theme's value on a live product page
recolours 2 of 378 elements, both headings, and moves zero geometry.
`test_patch_heading_contrast.py` 64 → 111: [L] covers every representative layout, and [N]
reproduces the defect on demand by narrowing the rule back to patch pages.

**PR** #152 · **Merge** `d0d829d1`

**Reopen only if** an AUXSAYS-owned fixed-dark layout again renders a general heading below 4.5:1

### AUX-013 — CI runs but does not gate merges

- **Status** Resolved · **Area** CI governance · **Severity** Medium
- **First seen** 2026-08-29 · **Resolved** 2026-10-07

**The revisit trigger fired, as itself.** This entry said *"Revisit trigger: a red CI run is
merged."* On 2026-10-07 ci-integrity run **37626793286** concluded `failure` at **13:29:27Z** for
PR #163 head `8ea5ce0d`, and **#163 merged at 13:29:46Z** — nineteen seconds later, by me, into
`main`. #164 then repaired `main` (run 37631143852, success). The cause was not a missed signal: the
status was read correctly and the merge was issued in the same uninterrupted command, so there was
no moment at which the red result could stop anything. **Discipline-dependent was not a mitigation.
It was the defect, and it had a nineteen-second half-life.**

**Symptom (original, still accurate)** The integrity workflow runs on every PR and fails on
check-count drift, but `main` is not branch-protected, so a red run blocks nothing.

#### Server-side enforcement was measured, not assumed

The previous deferral was *"blocked on dedicated app identity"*, carried as an assumption. It was
re-tested from scratch against a throwaway `aux-probe-protected` branch, with real pushes by real
`github-actions[bot]` runs. Every probe ruleset and the probe branch were deleted afterwards; the
repo ends this change with **0 rulesets and no branch protection**, exactly as it started.

| Probe | Ruleset | Bypass actors | `github-actions[bot]` fast-forward push |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | `pull_request` | none | **refused** — `GH013 ... Changes must be made through a pull request.` |
| B | `pull_request` | RepositoryRole admin(5) + write(2), `always` | **refused** — same `GH013` |
| C | `required_status_checks` (`integrity`) | none | **refused** — `Required status check "integrity" is expected.` |
| D | `pull_request` | **Integration** (GitHub Actions app, 15368) | **ruleset could not be created** — API `422`: *"Actor GitHub Actions integration must be part of the ruleset source or owner organization"* |

Repo state behind those results: `auxchan/auxsays.github.io` is public and owned by a **personal
`User` account**, so there is no owner organization for probe D to satisfy, and `OrganizationAdmin`
and `Team` bypass actors do not exist here either.

**One probe artifact worth recording, because it nearly produced the opposite conclusion.** The
first run of probe A reported a refusal that was not a rule at all: `! [rejected] (fetch first)`.
`actions/checkout` defaults to `fetch-depth: 1`, and a shallow clone cannot prove a fast-forward
against a tip it does not have, so **git refused the push locally before the server evaluated
anything**. Read uncritically it looks like proof that the bot is blocked. The probe was re-run with
`fetch-depth: 0` and a classifier that distinguishes a rule violation from any other refusal — only
then did the real `GH013` appear. A refusal is a property of the response, not of the rule.

**Conclusion.** Every ruleset shape that would refuse a red *human* merge also refuses the
`GITHUB_TOKEN` Actions identity, and **no bypass actor available on a personal-account repository
covers that identity**. Four workflows push generated records straight to `main` as that identity —
`davinci-updates.yml`, `obs-evidence-collection.yml`, `patch-ingest.yml`,
`promote-davinci-verified-reports.yml` — so server-side enforcement here is not a trade-off between
safety and convenience. It is evidence writeback, official ingestion, orchestration and the deploy
chain, against one class of human error. Restructuring those four lanes into pull requests was
measured and rejected: each writeback would have to pass the full 94-suite integrity run before its
own merge, and the bot cannot merge its own pull request under probe C either, so evidence would
strand instead of being refused. **Probe B is the part that inverts:** an admin bypass broad enough
to let a writer through is exactly broad enough to let the 13:29:46Z merge through, because the
identity that merged #163 is the repository owner.

#### Resolution: a repo-owned gate, because the merge path is the only part this repo owns

`auxsays/scripts/ci/guarded_merge.py`. It takes a PR number and refuses unless the required run is
green **for that pull request's exact current head SHA**:

- runs are fetched **by SHA, never by branch**, and each run's own `head_sha` is compared again
  locally — a query parameter is a request, not a guarantee;
- the governed workflow is pinned by **path** (`.github/workflows/ci-integrity.yml`), not by its
  display name, so renaming the workflow cannot silently empty the required set;
- **absence is a refusal.** No run, only another workflow's run, or only a run belonging to an
  earlier SHA all return `no_required_run`. A missing check is never green;
- a red run for the commit can never be outvoted by dispatching a greener one;
- `cancelled` / `skipped` / `neutral` do not satisfy the gate on their own, and an unrecognised
  conclusion refuses rather than defaults;
- a **failing** run vetoes whatever event produced it, while the **green** must be this pull
  request's own `pull_request` run -- otherwise cancelling the real run and dispatching the same
  workflow by hand would mint a pass;
- mergeability that GitHub has not computed yet is re-read a bounded number of times and then
  refuses under its own code, rather than being conflated with "not mergeable";
- the head **and the CI result** are both re-read immediately before merging and the whole
  decision re-run against the fresh view -- a run can be re-run to red while its commit stands
  still -- and the merge passes that SHA to GitHub as a **lease**, so the server refuses with
  409 if the head moved in the window;
- there is exactly **one** call site that can merge, and **no override flag**. Reporting is the
  default; only `--merge` merges, and only through the gate.

`AGENTS.md` gains a `## Merging` section: do not use `gh pr merge`, use this.

**Verification** `test_guarded_merge.py`, **82 checks**, offline, registered `[blocking]`. R1
replays the real recorded #163 head and run and asserts the gate refuses it; R2 enumerates all
nineteen refusals; R3 pins the single allow path and, deliberately, the cases that must *not* wedge
a good commit; R4 covers the read-to-merge window; R5 pins structure; R6 pins what the review below
found. **24 of 24 mutants killed.**

One of them mattered twice. The first version of the unrecognised-conclusion assertion passed with
its guard deleted, because a lone unknown conclusion is refused earlier for having no success and
never reaches that branch; it is pinned now with a green run beside it. Then the mutation harness
itself reported **22/22 while silently never running two mutants** — one mutant made the gate spin,
the timeout aborted the whole loop, and an empty survivor list printed as a clean sweep. The timeout
is per-mutant now. A test that cannot fail and a proof that cannot report a gap are the same shape
of problem as a CI run that cannot block.

#### What one bounded adversarial review changed

Six lenses, run once, on the questions the sprint named. **Q4 — can the automation writer still
update `main`? — came back CLEAN**, which is the result that mattered most: this change adds no
ruleset and no protection, touches none of the four writeback workflows, none of their tokens and
not `automation_writeback.py`, and the new suite is offline and writes nothing.

The other five found real defects, and they shared a theme. The gate was asking a **narrower
question than it claimed**, and its own command line could re-point it at an easier one.

- **`--workflow` was the override flag.** The module docstring said there was none. But
  `ci-integrity.yml` is the only workflow in this repo with a `pull_request:` trigger, so every
  other workflow is green on every commit by virtue of never having run on it — and
  `--workflow .github/workflows/pages.yml` produced an ALLOW line byte-identical to an honest one.
  `--workflow`, `--base` and `--repo` are gone; the CLI is a pull request number and `--merge`.
- **A hand-dispatched run counted as the pull request's check.** `event` was never read. Let the
  real run go red, cancel it (an ordinary habit for a run believed hung — `cancelled` is merely
  inconclusive, not a veto), then `gh workflow run ci-integrity.yml --ref <branch>`: a dispatched
  green for the same SHA, and the gate allowed. Red and green are now read **asymmetrically** — a
  failing run vetoes whatever event produced it, a green must be this pull request's own
  `pull_request` run.
- **A sibling pull request's run counted.** Two pull requests can share a head branch with different
  bases; only one of them was actually tested. A run that names pull requests must now name this one.
- **The pre-merge re-read refreshed only half the view.** The head was re-read; the CI result was
  not. A run can be re-run to red while its commit stands still, which is the same window shape
  again. Both halves are re-read now.
- **A truncated run page read as a complete one.** `total_count` was discarded, and the list is
  newest-first, so truncation drops the OLDEST runs — exactly where an earlier red would be. A
  partial answer now raises rather than being treated as an answer.
- **A cold read refused a perfectly good pull request.** GitHub computes `mergeable` asynchronously
  and answers `null` until it has, which is the usual answer on a freshly pushed head. The gate
  refused outright — which is fail-closed, and also precisely the experience that teaches an
  operator to go back to `gh pr merge`. It now re-reads a bounded number of times, and
  `mergeability_unknown` is its own refusal code rather than being conflated with "not mergeable".
- **A rejected lease exited 2.** The server declining the merge is a refusal, not this script
  malfunctioning. It exits 1 and reports the server's reason.
- **The rule was unreachable from the file that actually auto-loads.** `CLAUDE.md` is what Claude
  Code injects, and it said nothing about merging and never pointed at `AGENTS.md`. It does now.
**Three findings were accepted as residuals rather than fixed**, because each fix costs more than
the defect:

- **A pull request can edit the workflow that judges it.** For `pull_request` events GitHub
  evaluates the workflow file from the merge ref, so a change adding `continue-on-error: true` to
  the integrity step supplies its own green. True of PR-triggered CI generally, on any platform;
  closing it needs `pull_request_target` or a protected workflow, which is a different change.
- **`mergeable_state: "behind"` is allowed.** The base can move after CI ran, so the tested merge
  and the produced merge differ. In this repo `behind` is the *normal* state of any pull request
  more than a few hours old, because four workflows push to `main` all day — refusing it would mean
  rebasing before every merge. GitHub makes the strict version opt-in for the same reason. The gate
  reports `mergeable_state` in its ALLOW line rather than hiding it.
- **Nothing detects a merge that did not come through the gate.** A post-hoc detector is a separate
  workflow and a separate change.

**Honest residual.** This is not server-side enforcement and does not claim to be. `gh pr merge`,
the web merge button and a direct push to `main` all still work; what changed is that the correct
path is a single short command that cannot be talked into merging red, and that the repository now
says so where an agent reads its instructions. The demonstrated failure was a careless *shortcut*,
not a determined bypass, and a gate is a real fix for a shortcut.

**Reopen only if** a pull request is merged with a non-green required run for its head, or the gate
gains a path that merges without `decide()` returning an allow.

**Upgrade trigger (not a blocker)** If this repository ever moves to an organization, probes B and D
change answers: an org can name a GitHub App as an `Integration` bypass actor, and the same ruleset
that refuses a red human merge could then let the writeback identity through. At that point replace
this script with the ruleset and keep the script as the local pre-check.

### AUX-021 — Same-day releases on one servicing line were ordered by array position

- **Status** Resolved · **Area** Presentation · **Severity** High
- **First seen** 2026-09-30 · **Resolved** 2026-09-30

**Symptom** Windows 11 26H2 published `26300.9457` and `26300.9550` on the same day, 2026-09-29.
Both are official-source records and both are correct. `installedState` computed "newer" as
`recs.slice(0, index)` -- array position -- and `recs` is built by

the corpus produced a tie, position stood in for chronology.

have had to assert an order nobody published. It names no target and is not counted as "newer
available". The tied set reuses the existing `asSequence` / `step.tied` / `runOpeners` semantics, so
the same-day notice still opens once per run, and the steps list's left-hand connector -- which
reads as "this follows that" -- is masked at the tied boundary only. No build-number comparison and
no version arithmetic: a tie is a tie.

Explicitly unchanged: a strictly later build on the reader's line still produces `UPDATE AVAILABLE`;
a release on another Windows line is still a parallel sibling, not a target; Windows stays the
parallel-lines product and PowerPoint stays sequential; the beta/stable protections are untouched.

#### Health semantics: a method that stops running must not leave a frozen failure behind

`upsert_method_health` keys rows on `(product_id, update_version, target_build, method_id)` and
**retains whatever the current run did not emit.** Nothing ages, prunes or expires a row. So simply
routing six methods off would have left six `blocked` rows in the telemetry forever, with a frozen
`last_run`, still driving public output — `apply_consensus_to_records` reads those statuses with no
freshness check at all.

This is not hypothetical. **Acrobat already retired `adobe_community_search` and `reddit_search`, and
286 of their `blocked`/`broken` rows are still in the file**, newest `last_run` 2026-09-01, beside

unavailable during the last check" on Acrobat records today. The retiring comment there claims their
"health rows stay honest"; measured against the live file, they do not.

So every method routing declines to call still writes a row each cycle, with status **`disabled`** and
a fresh `last_run`. `disabled` is canonical, already means exactly this in the schema ("implemented,
deliberately switched off"), and is already emitted in production by three collectors across 167 rows,
so nothing new was invented. The not-run rows are built by their own function rather than by the
collector's status mapper, because that mapper returns `no_results` for a method with no candidates
and no errors — and the monitoring ladder counts `no_results` as **healthy**. Routing a method off
through the mapper would have published a method that never ran as a working source.

### AUX-028 — OBS had one evidence family and a reserved slot that discovered nothing

- **Status** Resolved · **Area** Evidence coverage · **Severity** Medium
- **First seen** 2026-10-09 · **Resolved** 2026-10-09

**Symptom** OBS Studio evidence came from exactly one source family, `obsproject/obs-studio`
GitHub Issues. The second method registered against the product, `known_watchlist`, emitted
`disabled` with `no_obs_watchlist_configured`, and its own note conceded it *"does not discover
candidates in Phase A."* Two method rows, one corpus. A GitHub outage took OBS evidence to zero
with nothing behind it.

**What a second family had to be.** Independence is by FAMILY, not by method count: a second
GitHub Issues query against the same repository is the same authors, the same platform and the
same moderation, renamed. Three candidates were measured in one bounded pass.

| candidate | verdict | measured reason |
|---|---|---|
| Reddit | **rejected** | `robots.txt` is `User-agent: *` / `Disallow: /`. Policy, not transport. |
| GitHub Discussions | **rejected** | Enabled, but 312 discussions across Announcements / Development Questions / General / Polls / RFPs. No user bug-report territory, and "Development Questions" is content the authority already refuses. Same platform as Issues. |
| **obsproject.com forum** | **ACTIVE** | 200, no auth. Three support subforums: Windows (32), Mac (33), Linux (34). |

**Robots decides the shape.** `/forum/list/` and `/forum/threads/` are allowed; `/forum/search/`,
`/forum/whats-new/`, `/forum/find-new/` and `/forum/posts/` are not. So the forum is
**enumerated, never queried** -- the same conclusion the PowerPoint source-coverage sprint
reached. And because a reply's canonical URL is `/forum/posts/<id>/`, which is disallowed, only
**opening posts** are read. That is the safe reading anyway: a reply must never inherit the
thread author's version, and reading only the first post makes that structural instead of a rule
someone has to remember.

**One authority, two families.** Both methods call `collect_obs_reports.evaluate_issue` and
`collect_obs_reports.evidence_row`. `evidence_row` grew one optional `identity` argument
supplying row id, source type/name and URL; omitted, it reproduces the GitHub row byte for byte.
Nothing else differs, and `source_weight` is 1 on both. The forum pool is narrowed per version
with the authority's own `match_basis`, not a second containment test, because a second version
predicate is a second authority waiting to drift.

**One writer, so dedupe is structural.** Both families' accepted rows go through a single
`write_evidence` call, which suppresses on `(product, version, id)` and `(product, version, url)`
and updates its seen-sets as it walks the batch. A report discovered twice becomes duplicate
telemetry -- accepted minus added -- not a second counted row. `write_evidence` gained an
`added_out` out-parameter so each family's delta is attributed from the appends that actually
happened rather than re-derived; re-deriving the suppression rule is how two predicates drift
apart.

**Three defects found while building it, each measured before it was changed.**

1. **`exact_version_re` refused a version at the end of a sentence.** The right-hand guard was
   `(?![0-9.])`, so *"I recently updated to 32.2.2."* matched nothing. Costless on GitHub, where
   the issue template puts the version on its own line; fatal on a forum, where prose is the only
   carrier. The guard must exclude a LONGER version, so it now refuses a following digit, or a
   dot then a digit. Measured over **1,111 live OBS issues: 2 outcomes move, 0 acceptances
   lost.** One real report is recovered (#12506, *"This started for me with 31.1.0"*).
2. **The counterweight that fix required.** The second moved outcome, #13982, says *"This issue
   does not occur in version 32.2.2"* -- a version named as HEALTHY, which the old matcher
   refused only by accident. The WORKING cue set covered "X doesn't have this problem" (target
   first) but not the negation-first direction. `target_lacks_occurrence` closes it. **Measured
   alone across the same 1,111 issues it changes nothing** -- it is a counterweight, not a
   widening. R1 (a declared version is never vetoed) and R3 (an explicit affected cue wins) still
   sit above it, which is what lets obsproject.com thread 196545 -- *"I then downgraded to OBS
   32.1.2 ... the exact same issue happens"* -- still count for the version it rolled back to.
3. **The same regex shape is CORRECT in the declared-version tokenizer, and was left alone.** The
   consequences are asymmetric: R1 makes a declared version immune to every veto, so
   over-recognising there grants immunity, while under-recognising prose only drops a report.
   Relaxing it would read `32.0.0.r2.ga75fdd2-1` (an AUR git build, #12669) as a bare 32.0.0
   declaration, and the nixpkgs build reports #13971/#13972 as declarations of 32.1.2. A comment
   and a test now hold the asymmetry in place.

**Stickies are excluded at discovery, and that exclusion is load-bearing.** The five pinned
threads in Windows Support are *"Dropped frames/disconnecting/lag? Read this first!"*, *"High CPU
usage ... Read this first!"*, *"Laptop black screen when capturing"*, *"My stream lags/buffers
constantly"* and *"Please post a log with your issue"*. Every one is written in the vocabulary of
the problems it is about -- the first title alone carries three concrete-issue terms -- and
describes nobody's install. They also sit on page 1 of every subforum forever, so enumeration
meets them first on every run. XenForo groups them in their own container, so this is a
structural fact about the listing rather than a guess from the title. Methods may discover
differently; they may not judge differently, so nothing about acceptance changed for the threads
that do get through. A test asserts that the authority *would* have accepted the guide, which is
what records the exclusion as load-bearing.

**Page dates are last-activity; the opening post's own date is not.** Thread 8870 renders
`2013-11-16`, `2013-11-16`, `2025-11-24`. The date is read from the first `<time>` inside the
opening post's own `<article>`, and the post's extent is bounded to the next post's article tag
-- searching the rest of the page would silently read a REPLY's date whenever the opening post's
`<time>` moved. A thread whose opening post has no date is dropped rather than dated by its
reply, and one whose body markup moves parses with an EMPTY body rather than borrowing a reply's.

**Quoted text is stripped with a balanced scan, not a regex.** A non-greedy
`<blockquote>.*?</blockquote>` matches the OUTER open tag through the INNER close on a nested
quote, consuming the outer's opening tag and leaving the outer's own trailing text behind --
another member's version claim, now attributed to the quoting author. Iterating the regex does
not fix that, because the outer open tag is already gone. An unbalanced quote drops everything
from its opening tag, which loses some of the author's own words: the right direction to fail.

**known_watchlist is retired honestly, not deleted.** It emits `disabled` with
`retired_superseded_by_obs_forum` on every run. Deleting it would have frozen its last row
forever -- the AUX-026 defect -- because `upsert_method_health` retains what a run does not emit.

**Health is per family.** `collect_one` returns `github_status` / `github_error` separately from
the merged result, and `github_health` derives the GitHub row from the GitHub outcome alone.
Before the second family existed those were the same thing; afterwards, a run where GitHub 502s
and the forum returns three reports still exits 0, and one verdict for both would publish that
outage as success. A GitHub failure no longer discards the forum's work either -- that is the
entire point of having a second family. The forum reports `blocked` (unreachable), `broken`
(pages fetched, nothing mapped), `partial` (any page lost, unparsed or body-less), `success` or
`no_results`. The body-less counter exists because a bbWrapper class rename would otherwise
degrade the family to titles only while health kept reporting success.

**The adversarial review found three real defects, and one it could not safely fix.**

- **Three phrasings naming the version as HEALTHY were counted as affected.** *"32.2.2 was fine
  for me"*, *"No problems on 32.2.2"* and *"I never saw this happen in 32.2.2"*. The
  results-table cue needs a bracket, dash or colon delimiter and `target_works` needs the word
  "work", so a plain adjective said in prose reached acceptance. Closed by `target_said_fine`,
  `no_problem_on_target`, and widening `target_lacks_occurrence` to allow an intervening object.
  `target_said_fine` carries a `(?!\s+until\b)` guard, because *"32.2.2 was fine UNTIL I added a
  browser source"* is a regression report and deleting it is the failure this cue could cause.
  **Measured: of 1,111 live OBS issues exactly one changes, and only its REJECTION REASON
  (#13982 moves from the accidental `generic_or_no_concrete_issue` to the correct
  `version_reported_working`); 0 acceptances are lost. Across all 1,320 counted stored rows,
  every product, 0 rows are newly vetoed.** Counterweights, not a widening.
- **One thread could be stored twice under two URL shapes.** `row_identity` took the text after
  the last dot, so `/forum/threads/196568/` (no slug) yielded the whole path as the "id" --
  a row id containing slashes, and a URL key that did not match the slugged form, so both
  landed. Latent rather than live, because the listing regex only emits the dotted shape, but an
  identity gate should not depend on that. `thread_id()` now resolves both shapes to the same
  numeric id and returns **None** rather than guessing; a candidate with no resolvable id is
  refused as `unresolvable_thread_identity`, because a row whose identity was invented is worse
  than a report that was not counted.
- **Residual, deliberately not fixed: *"No crashes with 32.2.2 at all"* is still read as
  affected.** The cause is not a missing veto -- it is the AFFECTED cue `failure_then_target`
  matching "crashes with 32.2.2" and winning at R3 before any veto runs. The obvious repair,
  adding the failure cues to `_POLARITY_SENSITIVE`, is **unsafe and was tested as an idea rather
  than shipped**: `_POLARITY_INVERTER` matches bare `not`, and `does not work` is itself one of
  the AFFECTED failure phrases, so those cues would self-invert and stop detecting a large part
  of the real corpus. Fixing it properly means re-measuring every product's corpus, which is a
  different piece of work. **Reopen if** a counted row is ever found whose only version mention
  sits inside a negated-healthy noun phrase.

**A quantifier in the cue table was silently a tuple.** The cue patterns are f-strings, so
`{0,2}` is a replacement field: Python evaluated it as `(0, 2)` and rendered that as literal text,
leaving `(?:\w+\s+)(0, 2)?` -- a group demanding EXACTLY one intervening word. It is not a syntax
error and it broke no test, because the case under test had exactly one word. It shipped that way
through a full green suite and 29 killed mutants, and only became visible when the over-long line
was wrapped and the rendered pattern printed. The table's existing cues double the braces for
this reason; mine did not. The repair is pinned two ways -- the quantifier is exercised at 0, 1
AND 2 intervening words, so no single-word case can stand in for the range again, and the whole
cue table (19 patterns across all five groups) is scanned for the `(n, m)` artifact.
